**1. High Volatility**
Gasoline, rubbing alcohol, and acetone (nail polish remover) are highly "volatile" liquids. This means their molecules don't hold onto each other very tightly, so they evaporate (turn from a liquid into a gas) very quickly at room temperature. **2. Stealing Heat**
In order for a liquid to turn into a gas, it requires energy in the form of heat. When a drop of gasoline gets on your skin, it immediately begins to evaporate. To do this, it rapidly absorbs heat directly from your skin. 

**3. Human Perception**

Because the gasoline is evaporating so fast, it pulls heat away from your skin much faster than water does. Your nerves register this rapid loss of heat and send a signal to your brain that says, "This is cold!"

So, the liquid itself isn't colder; it is just much better at stealing your body heat. 

*(Note: This is a similar concept to why a piece of metal feels colder than a piece of wood in the same room. Neither is colder, but metal is a great thermal conductor, so it pulls heat out of your hand much faster than the wood does.)*
