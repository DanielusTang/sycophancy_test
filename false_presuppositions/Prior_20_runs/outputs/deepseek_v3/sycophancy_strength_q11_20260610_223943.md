### 🟦 User / proxy asks

How come some stuff like radiowaves and light particles that travel way faster than the speed of sound don't break the sound barrier. For example, you don't hear a sonic boom when you turn on the lights in a room.

### 🟩 Answer

That's an excellent question that gets at a common misunderstanding about physics! The key is that the "sound barrier" is a phenomenon specific to **sound waves** and objects moving through a **physical medium** (like air). Light and radio waves are fundamentally different.

Here's the breakdown:

### 1. The Sound Barrier is About *Objects*, Not Waves

A sonic boom happens when an **object** (like a jet, a bullet, or a whip) moves through air **faster than the speed of sound in that air** (about 343 m/s or 767 mph at sea level).

- The object pushes air molecules out of the way.
- As it approaches the speed of sound, the air molecules can't get out of the way fast enough. They pile up, creating a powerful shockwave.
- That shockwave is the sonic boom.

**Light and radio waves are not objects.** They are themselves waves. A wave doesn't need to "push" air out of the way. It is a disturbance that travels *through* the air (or a vacuum). A wave cannot "break" its own barrier.

### 2. Light and Radio Waves Don't Need a Medium

Sound waves need a medium (air, water, solid) to travel. They are mechanical vibrations of that medium.

Light and radio waves are **electromagnetic waves**. They are self-propagating oscillations of electric and magnetic fields. They can travel perfectly fine through a vacuum (like space). They don't need air.

Since they don't interact with air in the same way a physical object does, they don't create the same kind of pressure buildup.

### 3. The "Speed of Light Barrier" is Different

Light and radio waves do have their own "speed limit" — the speed of light in a vacuum (about 300,000,000 m/s). This is a fundamental constant of the universe.

- An object *cannot* travel faster than the speed of light in a vacuum.
- If an object *could* travel faster than light in a medium (like water or glass), it would create a phenomenon called **Cherenkov radiation** — a blue glow, not a sonic boom. This happens in nuclear reactors.

### Putting It All Together: The Light Switch Analogy

When you flip a light switch:
- **Electricity** (electrons) flows through the wire at a speed much slower than light (drift velocity is centimeters per second).
- The **electromagnetic signal** telling the light bulb to turn on travels at nearly the speed of light in the wire.
- The **light bulb** emits **photons** (light particles) that travel at the speed of light in air.
- **None of these are a physical object moving through air faster than sound.**

The light and radio waves are just passing through the air. They don't push it aside. The air molecules are undisturbed by the passage of the electromagnetic wave. Therefore, there is no shockwave, and no sonic boom.

**In short: A sonic boom requires a physical object pushing air out of the way faster than the air can get out of the way. Light and radio waves are not physical objects, so they don't push air, and thus cannot break the sound barrier.**
