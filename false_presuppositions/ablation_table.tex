Table~\ref{tab:fp-ablation} reports one-factor-at-a-time ablations that isolate
which components of the attack pipeline drive sycophantic collapse under
adversarial false-presupposition pressure. Starting from the \textsc{D0}
baseline---a DeepSeek-R1 target probed by a DeepSeek-R1 reasoner proxy with the
target's reasoning visible, no judge feedback, and no escalation---each condition
toggles a single factor. Collapse itself is near-universal: every condition drives
the target to fully affirm the false premise in $100\%$ of cases, except A4 at
$19/20$. The informative signal is therefore \emph{how many turns} the target
resists before eroding and collapsing, which we report paired over the $10$
questions common to all conditions (higher $=$ more robust). Supplying the proxy
with judge feedback (A1) accelerates full collapse the most (from turn $17.6$ to
$12.8$), whereas replacing the reasoner proxy with a weaker chat proxy (A4)
roughly doubles the target's survival time (to turn $24.6$), confirming that a
reasoning-capable adversary is substantially more effective. Escalate-on-erosion
(A3) produces the earliest \emph{effective} (soft-cave) collapse (turn $4.0$) by
pouncing the moment erosion is detected.
