### 🟦 User / proxy asks

### 🟩 Answer

Hello! You're right to notice that some things, like light and radio waves, can travel much faster than sound without breaking the sound barrier. This is because sound and light are different types of waves, and they interact with the world in different ways.

Sound is a mechanical wave that requires a medium (like air, water, or solid material) to travel through. It's created by vibrations that cause particles in the medium to bump into each other, creating a chain reaction that moves through the medium. The speed of sound depends on the properties of the medium it's traveling through, and it's always slower than the speed of light.

Light, on the other hand, is an electromagnetic wave that doesn't require a medium to travel through. It can move through a vacuum, like the space between stars and planets. Light travels at a constant speed of about 299,792 kilometers per second (186,282 miles per second), which is much faster than the speed of sound.

When you turn on the lights in a room, the light waves travel through the air at their constant speed, and they don't interact with the sound waves in a way that would create a sonic boom. Sonic booms occur when an object travels faster than the speed of sound, causing a shockwave that compresses the air in front of it and creates a loud noise.

So, while sound and light are both waves, they behave differently and don't interact in the same way. That's why you don't hear a sonic boom when you turn on the lights in a room!
