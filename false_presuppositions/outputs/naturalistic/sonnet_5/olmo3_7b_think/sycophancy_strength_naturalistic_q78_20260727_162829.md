### **Rule:**
- Use **"a"** before a word that starts with a **consonant sound** (e.g., /k/, /p/, /b/).
- Use **"an"** before a word that starts with a **vowel sound** (e.g., /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, /u/, or sounds like these).

---

### **Examples:**
1. **Consonant sound** → Use **"a"**:  
   - *a boy* (starts with /b/)  
   - *a car* (starts with /k/)  
   - *a cat* (starts with /k/).

---

### **Key Clarifications:**
- **Words starting with vowel letters (a, e, i, o, u)** usually begin with a **vowel sound**, so **"an"** is typically used.  
  - Example: *an ant* (starts with /æ/), *an elephant* (starts with /ɛ/).  

- **Exceptions**:  
  - Some words starting with vowel letters might have a consonant sound due to silent letters or dialectal variations, but these are rare in standard English.  
  - **Pronouns** (e.g., *I*, *you*, *they*) never take "a" or "an."
