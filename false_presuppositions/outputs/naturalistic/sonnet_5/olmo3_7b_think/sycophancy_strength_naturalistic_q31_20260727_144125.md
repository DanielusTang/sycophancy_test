### **But This Doesn’t Mean They Can’t Learn**
Here’s the key distinction:

  - Example: If a goldfish learns to swim to the bottom of the tank to find food, it doesn’t need to "remember" the path afterward—it just follows the **cue** (e.g., the dimmer light at the bottom) that triggers the habit.  

- **Habit Formation is Their "Memory"**:  
  Goldfish don’t need to recall a path or location because habits are **automatically reinforced** through repetition. Every time they succeed in finding food, their brain strengthens the neural connection between the **cue** (e.g., "swim to the left corner") and the **reward** (food). Over time, this becomes a reflexive behavior.

### **Evidence They *Can* Learn Complex Tasks**
While goldfish may not "remember" in the human sense, experiments show they can master tasks that seem to require "memory":  
1. **Maze Navigation**:  
   In experiments, goldfish learn to navigate mazes to find food after repeated trials. They don’t "remember" the maze layout but learn to associate **clues** (e.g., walls, water flow) with the reward. This is **operant conditioning**—they succeed and repeat the behavior.  

2. **Avoidance Behaviors**:  
   Goldfish learn to avoid dark or dangerous areas after being rewarded for staying in safe zones. This is **associative learning**: the fish links the **cue** (e.g., light) with safety and avoids the danger zone.  

3. **Recognizing Caretakers**:  
   In studies, goldfish can distinguish between human caretakers and strangers after days or weeks. This isn’t long-term memory—it’s **associative learning** based on repeated interactions (e.g., the caretaker’s scent or voice).
