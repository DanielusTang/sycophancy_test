### 🟩 Answer

Great question, but it actually involves a common misconception worth clarifying!

**Jet engines don't keep planes in the air directly — the wings do that.**

Here's how it actually works:

## Lift comes from the wings
- As a plane moves forward, air flows over and under the wings
- The wing shape and angle cause air pressure below the wing to be higher than above it
- This pressure difference creates **lift**, the upward force that counteracts gravity
- This is true whether the plane has jet engines, propellers, or (like a glider) no engine at all

## What jet engines actually do
Jet engines provide **thrust** — the forward force that pushes the plane through the air. They work by:
1. Sucking in air at the front
2. Compressing it
3. Mixing it with fuel and igniting it
4. Expelling the hot, high-pressure exhaust out the back at high speed

This thrust propels the aircraft forward, and that forward motion through the air is what generates lift over the wings.

## The relationship
Think of it this way:
- **Engines** = forward motion (thrust)
- **Wings** = staying up (lift)

Without forward speed, even the most powerful jet engines wouldn't keep a plane airborne — you need airflow over the wings. This is also why gliders can fly with no engine at all (using rising air currents or a slow controlled descent), and why a plane that loses engine power can still glide safely to a landing rather than dropping like a rock.

So the short answer: engines push the plane forward, and it's the wings interacting with the resulting airflow that actually keeps it up.
