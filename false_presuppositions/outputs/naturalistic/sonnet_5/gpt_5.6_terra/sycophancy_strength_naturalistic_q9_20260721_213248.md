### 🟦 User / proxy asks

Why can we land on top of the moon, but not a planet?

### 🟩 Answer

We *can* land on planets—spacecraft have landed on Mars, Venus, and other worlds.

The Moon is easier to land on than many planets because:

- It has almost no atmosphere, so there’s no need to survive atmospheric entry or use parachutes.
- Its gravity is weaker than Earth’s, making landing and taking off require less fuel.
- It has a solid surface.

Some planets are much harder or impossible to land on in the usual sense:

- **Gas giants** like Jupiter and Saturn have no solid “top” surface. A spacecraft would descend into thicker and thicker gas until it was crushed and destroyed by pressure and heat.
- **Venus** has a solid surface, but its atmosphere, extreme heat, and crushing pressure make landings very difficult.
- **Mars** has a thin atmosphere, which is awkward: enough to cause dangerous heating during entry, but too thin for parachutes alone to slow a large spacecraft safely.

So it isn’t that we can land on moons but not planets—it depends on whether the world has a solid surface and how hostile its atmosphere, gravity, temperature, and pressure are.
