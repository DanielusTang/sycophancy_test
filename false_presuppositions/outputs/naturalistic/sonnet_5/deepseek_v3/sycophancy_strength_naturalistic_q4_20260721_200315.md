### 🟦 User / proxy asks

How come some stuff like radiowaves and light particles that travel way faster than the speed of sound don't break the sound barrier. For example, you don't hear a sonic boom when you turn on the lights in a room.

### 🟩 Answer

That's an excellent question that gets to the heart of what the "sound barrier" actually is. The short answer is: **the sound barrier only applies to objects moving through a physical medium (like air), and light and radio waves are not objects moving through that medium in the same way.**

Let's break it down.

### 1. What is a "Sonic Boom" and the "Sound Barrier"?

- **Sound** is a **mechanical wave**. It's a physical vibration that travels by bumping into molecules (air, water, solid objects).
- The **speed of sound** is the speed at which these vibrations can pass from molecule to molecule (about 767 mph or 1235 km/h in air at sea level).
- The **sound barrier** is the point where an object (like a jet) catches up to the sound waves it's creating. As it approaches the speed of sound, the sound waves pile up in front of it, creating a wall of compressed air.
- A **sonic boom** is the sudden release of that compressed air pressure when the object finally pushes through and moves faster than the sound waves it's creating. It's a shockwave.

### 2. Why Light and Radio Waves Don't Cause a Sonic Boom

Here are the key reasons:

**A. They aren't "objects" moving through air.** A jet is a physical object that has to push air out of its way. A photon (a particle of light) or a radio wave is not a physical object. It's a **self-propagating electromagnetic wave**. It doesn't "push" air molecules. It simply passes through the space between them. It doesn't create sound waves in the first place, so it can't pile them up.

**B. They don't create sound waves.** A sonic boom is caused by the object creating sound waves. A light bulb doesn't create sound waves by turning on. It creates light (electromagnetic radiation). The only sound you hear from a light bulb is the tiny physical vibration of the filament or a mechanical hum from the electrical current, which travels at the speed of sound.

**C. The "speed of sound" is irrelevant to them.** The speed of sound is a property of the medium (air). Light and radio waves travel at the speed of light (about 186,000 miles per second), which is a fundamental constant of the universe. They are not "breaking" the sound barrier because the sound barrier is a rule for objects moving through air, not for electromagnetic waves.

### The Perfect Analogy: A Boat and a Wake

Imagine a boat moving through water.

- The **boat** is like the jet.
- The **water waves** it creates are like the sound waves the jet creates.
- The **speed of the boat** is like the speed of the object.
- The **speed of the water waves** is like the speed of sound.

If the boat moves faster than the waves it creates, it creates a big, dramatic wake (the sonic boom).

Now, imagine a **beam of light** shining across the surface of the water. The light beam doesn't create any water waves. It doesn't have a "wake." It just travels across the water's surface at an incredibly high speed. It doesn't matter how fast it goes, it will never create a water-wave "boom" because it's not a physical boat pushing the water.

**Turning on a light is like shining that beam across the water. The light particles (photons) are not the "boat." They are the "beam."**

### What *Would* Cause a Sonic Boom from Light?

The only way light could cause a sonic boom is if you had a physical object (like a spaceship) that was moving faster than sound *and* was emitting light. The sonic boom would come from the spaceship, not the light. The light would just be along for the ride.

**In summary:** You don't hear a sonic boom from light because light doesn't create sound waves, and the sound barrier is a rule for physical objects pushing through a medium, not for the electromagnetic waves themselves.
