### 🟦 User / proxy asks

Even though 72 is room temperature year round, why does it feel colder in the winter?

### 🟩 Answer

Great question! The key here is understanding how our bodies perceive temperature and how the environment affects us.

**Room temperature**—like 72°F (22°C)—is considered comfortable for most people in a controlled indoor setting. However, when it's winter outside, even if the air temperature is the same as in summer, it *feels* much colder in the winter for several reasons:

### 1. **Lower Solar Radiation (Less Heat from the Sun)**
In winter, the sun is lower in the sky (especially in the Northern Hemisphere), so its rays are more slanted and the amount of energy it provides to the ground is reduced. This means the ground and the air don't warm up as efficiently as in summer.

### 2. **Reduced Insulation by the Atmosphere**
In winter, the air tends to be drier and colder, and there's often more atmospheric pressure. This means the air doesn't trap as much heat from your body as it does in summer, when humidity is higher and air is warmer.

### 3. **Wind Chill**
Even without an actual temperature drop, wind can make it feel colder. In winter, there's often more wind or it's colder air moving past you, increasing the rate at which your body loses heat to the environment (a phenomenon called "wind chill," though it's not a real measure of temperature, just a sensation).

### 4. **Human Metabolism and Blood Flow**
Your body generates heat internally through metabolism. In winter, your body may work harder to maintain core temperature because it's losing heat faster to the cold environment, making the cold sensation more noticeable.

### 5. **Psychological Factors**
Our perception of temperature can be influenced by expectations. We're adapted to expect it to be colder in winter, so our brains interpret the same number (like 72°F) as feeling much colder.

---

**In summary:**  
Even though 72°F is the same number whether it's winter or summer, in winter the air holds less heat, the sun heats the ground less, and wind can make it feel like a much bigger drop from our warm bodies. That's why it feels so much colder outside in winter, even at the same temperature as in milder months.
