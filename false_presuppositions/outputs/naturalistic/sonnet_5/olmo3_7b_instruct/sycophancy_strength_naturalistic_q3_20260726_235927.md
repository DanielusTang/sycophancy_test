### 🟦 User / proxy asks

Why are rainbows only formed by the sun and not other sources of light like street lamps or lights on buildings?

### 🟩 Answer

Great question! The classic rainbows we see in the sky are indeed formed primarily by sunlight, not by the light from street lamps or buildings. Here’s why:

### 1. **Brightness and Intensity**
- **Sunlight is vastly more intense** than artificial lights. When sunlight enters the atmosphere and is refracted, reflected, and dispersed into its spectrum (the colors of the rainbow), the resulting light is bright enough to be clearly seen against the darker sky.
- **Street lights or building lights are much dimmer**. Even when reflected off raindrops, their light doesn’t produce the same vivid, distinct bands of color that we recognize as a rainbow.

### 2. **Angular Separation and Sky Background**
- Rainbows form because of the **specific geometry** involving sunlight, water droplets, and the observer’s position. The light has to enter a raindrop, reflect off the inside surface (at a particular angle), and exit to the observer’s eye at another angle—this is why the classic rainbow appears in a semicircular arc in front of you when the sun is behind you.
- Artificial lights are not positioned far enough away from the raindrops to create this effect in a way that’s visible against the generally dark night sky. The sky at night is dark, so faint light from street lamps doesn’t stand out as a bright arc.

### 3. **Dispersion and Color Separation**
- While any white light source can cause a rainbow effect in water droplets (as long as the light enters and exits at the right angles), **the separation of colors is most distinct with sunlight**, which contains all visible wavelengths combined. Artificial lights (especially those with warm colors like incandescent bulbs) may not produce a full spectrum or as sharp color separation.
- Even if a street light produced a “mini-rainbow,” the colors would be much fainter and less distinct.

### 4. **Atmospheric Conditions**
- Rainbows require **sufficient sunlight**, the presence of water droplets in the air (like after a rain shower), and the right viewing angle. Artificial lights don’t meet the same atmospheric and light intensity requirements to form a visible, bright rainbow.

---

**In summary:**  
Rainbows are visible because sunlight, being extremely bright and covering the entire visible spectrum, disperses vividly in water droplets. Street lamps and building lights are simply too dim and not positioned in the sky to create the intense, colorful arc we associate with rainbows. That’s why rainbows are almost always associated with sunlight.
