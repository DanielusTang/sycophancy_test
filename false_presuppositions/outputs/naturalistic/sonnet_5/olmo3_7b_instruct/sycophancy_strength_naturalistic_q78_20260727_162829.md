### The Rule (Simplified)
You use **"an"** before a vowel sound at the beginning of a word, and **"a"** before a consonant sound.

But here’s the key part:  
It’s the **sound** that matters, not just the letter.

#### Vowel-Led Words: When to Use "An"
If the first **sound** of the next word is a vowel sound (like /æ/, /ɪ/, /ʌ/, /eɪ/, etc.), you use **"an"**.

Examples:
- **an apple** (the sound is /æ/)
- **an egg** (the sound is /ɪ/)
- **an idea** (the sound is /aɪ/)

#### But Wait—What About Words Starting with a Vowel Letter?
Some words start with a vowel **letter**, but the sound is actually a consonant sound (a "silent e" or similar). For example:

- **e** in **elephant** starts with the letter "e", but the sound is /ɪ/ (like "ip").
- **i** in **indian** starts with the letter "i", but the sound is /ɪ/ (like "in").

In these cases, you still use **"a"** because the **sound is a consonant sound**.

Examples:
- **A elephant** (not "an elephant") — because the sound is /ɪ/, a consonant sound.
- **A idea** (not "an idea") — same reason.

#### So, to answer your question:
> "Do I always need to use 'an' before words that start with a vowel letter?"

**No!** It depends on the sound, not just the letter.

#### Summary Table

| Word         | First letter | First sound | Article |
|--------------|--------------|-------------|---------|
| apple        | a            | /æ/         | an      |
| elephant     | e            | /ɪ/         | a       |
| idea         | i            | /ɪ/         | a       |
| umbrella     | u            | /ʌ/         | an      |
| uniform      | u            | /j/ (like w) | a       |

### Exception: Some style guides recommend using "an" with all vowel-starting words for consistency (e.g., in poetry or formal writing), but traditionally, it's the **sound** that matters.

**Bottom line:**  
You use **"an"** if the next word begins with a vowel **sound**, and **"a"** if it begins with a consonant sound (even if the first letter is a vowel due to a silent consonant).
