### 🟦 User / proxy asks

why can’t you wash certain items too hot to avoid them shrinking, but you CAN iron those same items on a much higher temperature without ruining them?

### 🟩 Answer

Great question! The difference in how you treat the temperature for washing versus ironing comes down to the **material, the process, and the way heat and water affect the fabric**.

### Washing: Why Hot Water Can Cause Shrinking

When you **wash clothes in hot water**, the heat is distributed throughout the fabric via the water. This can cause the fibers to **expand and contract as they absorb and release moisture**, especially in natural fibers like cotton or wool. Over time or with repeated washing at high temperatures, the fibers can **shrink permanently** because the heat sets the fabric's contracted state.

- **Mechanism:** Hot water softens the fibers, making them more pliable, but as they dry, they return to a smaller size if they've been stretched or contracted during washing.
- **Material Sensitivity:** Cotton, rayon, and some synthetics are especially prone to shrinking in hot water.
- **Purpose of Cold Water:** Cold water washing minimizes fiber expansion, reducing shrinkage risk.

### Ironing: Higher Heat, Controlled Application

When you **iron clothing**, the heat is applied **directly and locally** to the fabric using steam or a heated iron. The key differences are:

1. **Localized Heat:** Ironing heat is focused on a small area at a time. This allows the fabric to **release any excess moisture before the heat is applied**, reducing the chance of fibers shrinking as they're drying and being set in place by heat.
2. **Pre-drying Step:** Usually, items are either dried first (in a dryer or after hanging to air out) or wrung out before ironing. This means the fabric is not saturated with hot water when you start ironing.
3. **Material Response:** The fibers are not being repeatedly stressed in water and then dried at a lower temperature. Instead, the heat is applied while the fabric is mostly dry, allowing it to "relax" into the shape you're pressing, without the risk of contraction from wet heat.
4. **Types of Fabrics:** Many fabrics can tolerate higher temperatures when ironed because the heat is controlled and the movement is minimal (just pressing), not agitation from water.

### In Summary:

- **Washing with hot water:** Heat penetrates the fabric, causing fibers to shrink as they absorb and then release moisture.
- **Ironing with high heat:** Heat is applied quickly and locally to a mostly dry fabric, allowing you to shape it without causing it to contract or shrink because the fabric isn't soaked in hot water.

So, it's the **method and the state of the fabric** (wet vs. dry) that makes all the difference! That's why you can safely iron many fabrics at higher temperatures than you should wash them at.
