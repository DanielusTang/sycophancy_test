### 🟦 User / proxy asks

Why are waterproof phones rated for time periods submerged etc... (ie, 5m for 30 mintues). Doesnt waterproof mean water does not get in? Why is there a time limit.

### 🟩 Answer

Great question! Let’s break it down:

### What Do "Waterproof" Ratings Mean?

When a phone is labeled as "water-resistant" or "waterproof" (usually with ratings like IP68 or IPX7), it means it has been tested to withstand certain amounts of water exposure under specific conditions. These ratings **do not mean the phone is completely sealed from water forever**—they're about how long and under what circumstances the phone can survive water exposure.

### Why Are There Time Limits (Like 30 Minutes)?

#### 1. **Real-World Use vs. Laboratory Testing**
- **Testing Conditions Are Controlled:** Phone manufacturers test devices in controlled environments (like a special chamber) with exact amounts of water, at specific temperatures, and under precise methods (such as submerging the phone head-first or on its side).
- **Real-World Scenarios Vary:** In the wild, water can get *in* through ports, speakers, buttons, or any openings. Even a "waterproof" phone can still have vulnerable areas that let water in over time if, for example, you drop it in a puddle or let it sit in the rain for too long.

#### 2. **Material Degradation**
- **Seals Can Fail Over Time:** The gaskets and seals that keep water out aren't infinitely durable. With prolonged exposure to water, they can degrade, allowing water to eventually enter.
- **Corrosion and Damage:** Staying submerged too long can cause corrosion inside the device, which can damage sensitive components like the motherboard, battery, or connectors.

#### 3. **Manufacturer Guidelines**
- The time limit (like 30 minutes at 1.5 meters deep for IP68) is a **conservative guideline** set by testing standards (like IEC 61868 for IP ratings). It's to account for worst-case scenarios and to give consumers a realistic expectation of how the device will perform in practice.

### Why Doesn't It Mean "Completely Water-Proof"?

- **No Device Is Truly "Water-Proof":** Even the most expensive "waterproof" phones (like those used in underwater photography) are not designed to be fully submerged indefinitely or to survive being dropped onto a wet surface.
- **There Are Always Gaps:** There are usually small openings for charging ports, speakers, and microphones which can allow water in, especially if the phone is not properly sealed or if it's turned the wrong way when submerged.

### In Summary

**Waterproof ratings set time limits because:**
- Real-world exposure is messier and riskier than controlled lab tests.
- Seals and materials aren't indestructible.
- Prolonged or repeated exposure can cause internal damage.
- The time and depth limits are set to reflect a reasonable expectation of safety for typical mishaps.

So, while a phone might survive 30 minutes underwater, using it beyond the recommended time or in harsh conditions (like high pressure or prolonged submersion) increases the risk of failure. That’s why manufacturers include time limits in their ratings.
