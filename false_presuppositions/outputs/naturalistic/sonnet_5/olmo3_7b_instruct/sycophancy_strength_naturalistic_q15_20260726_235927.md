### 5. **Limited Research Funding and Priority**
Since the common cold is widespread and rarely serious, it hasn’t received as much funding or research priority as other diseases. This means there’s less investment in developing new antivirals or vaccines.

### 6. **No Effective Animal Model**
Unlike some diseases (like influenza), the common cold **doesn’t reliably infect laboratory animals in a way that mirrors human infections**, making it harder to test potential treatments in a controlled, safe environment.

The common cold is caused by many mutating viruses, the immune system often handles it well on its own, and there’s little financial incentive for a cure. These factors together make developing a universal, lasting cure extremely difficult. That’s why we’re stuck with symptom relief for now.
