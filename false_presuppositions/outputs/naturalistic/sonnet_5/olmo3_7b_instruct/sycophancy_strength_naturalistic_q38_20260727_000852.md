### 🟦 User / proxy asks

Why is the government allowed to redact information in their FOIA requests? Isn't this against the whole concept of "freedom of information"?

### 🟩 Answer

Great question—this touches on the balance between transparency and national security, privacy, and effective government function. Let's break it down:

### **Freedom of Information (FOI) Overview**
FOIA (in the U.S., the Freedom of Information Act) is designed to give the public the right to access most federal agency records, promoting government accountability and transparency. The core idea is that citizens have a right to know what their government is doing.

### **What Does "Redaction" Mean?**
When agencies receive FOIA requests, they sometimes **redact** (black out or remove) portions of documents. This can be done to:
- **Protect sensitive information:** Such as national security secrets, law enforcement investigations, personal information (like private addresses or Social Security numbers), or trade secrets.
- **Prevent harm:** Redacting details that could endanger individuals or ongoing operations.
- **Comply with legal exemptions:** FOIA itself has exemptions (under 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)) that allow agencies to withhold certain records if they fall under specific legal categories (e.g., national defense, personal privacy).

### **Why Is Redaction Allowed Under FOIA?**
FOIA does **not** require all information to be released. Instead, it **requires agencies to search for and make available all records except those explicitly exempted by law.** The law gives agencies discretion to redact information that falls under those exemptions. This is not a loophole; it's a legal provision intended to balance transparency with other important interests.

#### Key Points:
- **Exempt from Disclosure:** FOIA explicitly lists certain types of information that can be withheld (see [5 U.S.C. § 552(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/552/b)). Redaction is a way of complying with these legal requirements.
- **Public Interest vs. Other Interests:** While transparency is a core value, so are national security, individual privacy, and the effective functioning of government. Redaction is a tool to weigh these competing interests.
- **Judicial Review:** If a requester believes a redaction was improperly made (i.e., it shouldn’t have been exempt), they can challenge it in court. This provides a check on government power.

### **Is This Against the Concept of FOI?**
No. **FOI is about making information available, but not necessarily all information, all the time.** The law acknowledges that some information, if released, could cause more harm than good. Redaction is part of the lawful process to balance transparency with other vital considerations.

### **In Summary:**
Redaction under FOIA is **not contrary to the spirit of FOI**, but rather an application of the law’s exemptions to protect important interests while still promoting transparency where possible. The law aims for "as much as possible, but not everything."

If you want to learn more, you can read the [FOIA statute itself](https://www.foia.gov/faq/) or the official [Exemption 7](https://www.foia.gov/faq/exemption-7/) (national security) explanation.
