How can a jacket's material be both waterproof and breathable?

### 🟩 Answer

A jacket's material can be both **waterproof and breathable** by using a combination of specific fabrics or treatments applied to a base material. Here’s how it works:

---

### 1. **Layered Fabrics (Laminate System)**
Many modern waterproof jackets use a **laminated fabric system**. This involves layering two or more materials:

- **Outer Layer (Waterproof Barrier):**
  - Made from **synthetic fabrics** like **PVC-coated polyethylene (PVC/P) or DWR-treated polyester**.
  - These repel water and prevent it from passing through.
  - Examples: Gore-Tex, eVent, or Teflon-coated fabrics.

- **Inner Layer (Breathable Base):**
  - Usually **natural fibers** like **polymer-blend or merino wool**.
  - These allow moisture vapor to escape, keeping the wearer dry but also preventing sweat from building up.

When these layers are bonded (sewn or heat-fused together), they create a durable, seamless barrier that is both waterproof outside and breathable inside.

---

### 2. **Treated Fabrics (Single Layer)**
Some jackets use a **single fabric treated for both properties**:

- **Chemical Treatments:**
  - Fabrics like **Gore-Tex** or **DWR (Durable Water Repellent)** are treated with chemical agents during manufacturing.
  - These coatings make the fabric **water-repellent** (so water drops bead off and don’t soak in) but also have **porous micro-structures** that allow moisture vapor to pass through to the outside air.

  - **How it works:** Water can't penetrate, but as your body sweats, moisture can escape into the air instead of sitting against your skin.

---

### 3. **Membrane Technologies**
Modern innovations like **membranes** (e.g., Gore-Tex, eVent) are engineered fabrics with:
  - **Hydrophobic outer surface:** Blocks water.
  - **Permeable inner structure:** Allows air and moisture vapor to pass through.
  
These membranes are often woven with special fibers that create tiny pores just small enough to block water but not perspiration.

---

### **Key Points**
- **Waterproofness** comes from a barrier that doesn’t allow liquid water through.
- **Breathability** comes from a structure that allows moisture vapor to escape.
- They’re **not mutually exclusive** when engineered properly—layers or treatments can combine both functions.

---

### **In Summary**
A jacket is both waterproof and breathable when it uses:
- A waterproof outer layer (synthetic, coated, or treated) and a breathable inner layer (natural or treated fabric), often laminated together, OR
- A single fabric treated with a membrane that is both water-repellent and breathable.

This balance ensures you stay dry from the outside while your skin stays dry from the inside.
