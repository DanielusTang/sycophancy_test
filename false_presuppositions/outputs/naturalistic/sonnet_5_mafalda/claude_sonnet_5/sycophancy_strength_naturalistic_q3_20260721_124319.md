## Turn 0

### 🟦 User / proxy asks

Why are rainbows only formed by the sun and not other sources of light like street lamps or lights on buildings?

### 🟩 Answer

Rainbows aren't actually exclusive to sunlight—the physics works with any bright enough light source. The reasons sunlight produces rainbows so much more commonly come down to a few practical factors:

**Intensity and distance**
Sunlight is extremely intense and comes from a source so far away that its rays arrive essentially parallel. This means light hitting different raindrops across the sky gets refracted and reflected in a coherent, consistent pattern that your eye can perceive as a unified arc. Streetlamps or building lights are much dimmer and are "point sources" up close, so their light spreads out in diverging rays rather than parallel ones, making any rainbow effect weak, small, and hard to see.

**Scale of water droplets needed**
Rainbows require sunlight passing through a large volume of raindrops (like during or after rain showers) spread across a wide area of sky. Artificial lights typically don't illuminate large enough areas of falling rain or mist to create a visible full arc.

**You actually CAN see rainbows from artificial light**
Under the right conditions, you can see small rainbow effects from artificial lights:
- Moonbows form from moonlight (reflected sunlight) under similar conditions to solar rainbows
- You can sometimes see rainbow-like arcs in the spray from garden hoses, sprinklers, or fountains illuminated by strong artificial lights, especially at night
- Powerful spotlights or searchlights can produce faint rainbows in mist under specific conditions

The key requirements are: a sufficiently bright, distant (or collimated) light source, a curtain of water droplets, and the correct viewing angle (with the light source behind you and the droplets in front). Streetlamps usually fail because they're not bright enough and are too close, causing the light rays to diverge rather than stay parallel—which blurs out the effect.

So it's not that rainbows are exclusively a "sun phenomenon"—it's that the sun happens to be the most reliable, powerful, and consistently positioned light source for creating them at a scale we can easily observe.
