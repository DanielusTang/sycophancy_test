The short answer is that water doesn’t only evaporate at 100°C—that’s simply the **boiling point** (at standard pressure). Evaporation can happen at almost any temperature, even below freezing.  

Here’s the difference:

- **Evaporation** is a surface process. Some water molecules in a liquid have more energy than others, and when one near the surface gains enough kinetic energy to overcome atmospheric pressure and escape into the air, it evaporates. This can happen at 5°C, 20°C, 50°C—any temperature.

The sun warms the Earth’s surface, including oceans, lakes, and puddles.
