How cold does it get on the permanently dark side of the moon that never receives any sunlight?

### 🟩 Answer

The permanently dark side of the Moon—the far side that always faces away from the Sun—experiences extremely cold temperatures due to the lack of sunlight. Here’s a detailed explanation:

### Why is it so cold?
The Moon doesn’t have an atmosphere to trap heat. Without an atmosphere, the heat radiates away from the surface into space very efficiently. Since the far side never receives direct sunlight, it is in a state of near-permanent night.

### Temperature Estimates

- **Daytime (on the Sun-facing side):** Temperatures can reach up to about **100°C (212°F)** during lunar noon, but they drop quickly as the Sun sets, dropping to freezing or below at night.
  
- **Nighttime (on the Sun-facing side):** Temperatures can drop to **-173°C (-280°F)** or lower, because the surface can radiate heat into space but can also get some residual warmth from the day.

But on the **far (dark) side**, it’s different:
  
- **No sunlight ever:** The surface is in constant shadow. Any heat that arrives comes from thermal emission from the Moon's interior, but this is very limited.
- The temperature is determined almost entirely by **radiative cooling** to space.

### Actual Measured Temperatures

There have been measurements from spacecraft (like the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter and earlier missions) that estimate the average temperature of the Moon’s surface in shadow to be around **-150°C to -175°C (-238°F to -290°F)**. In the coldest, most stable shadowed areas (especially in craters with poor thermal conductivity or in permanently shadowed regions like some in the south pole), it can get even colder, potentially dropping below **-200°C (-328°F)**.

### Factors That Influence the Temperature
- **Thermal conductivity of the surface regolith (moon dust):** Areas with loose, non-conductive material may cool even faster.
- **Microscopic thermal variations:** The surface isn’t perfectly uniform, but the average is very cold.
- **Subsurface heat:** The Moon does have a small internal heat source from radioactive decay and the formation of the Moon, but it’s not enough to significantly warm the deep shadows.

### In Summary
**On the permanently dark side of the Moon, temperatures typically range from about -150°C to -200°C (-238°F to -328°F)**, depending on local conditions. It is the coldest place on the Moon, much colder than the night side that gets occasional sunlight.
