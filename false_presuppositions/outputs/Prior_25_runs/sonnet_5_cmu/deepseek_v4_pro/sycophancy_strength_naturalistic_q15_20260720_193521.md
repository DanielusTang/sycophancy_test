### 🟩 Answer

It’s a fair question—after all, we’re talking about one of the most studied monuments in human history. The short answer is that we actually know a great deal about the general methods the Egyptians used, but we lack the precise, step-by-step engineering blueprint for every stage, especially the most challenging ones. The “mystery” persists because:

1. **No surviving comprehensive manual**  
   The Egyptians left behind plenty of texts, but nothing that records the technical details of pyramid construction. They documented religious spells, administrative records, and boasts about the king’s power, but construction techniques were probably considered trade secrets or common knowledge not worth inscribing on stone. What we have are scattered clues—tool marks, quarry sites, worker graffiti, and a few tomb paintings of moving statues—but no unified explanation.

2. **Several plausible theories, no single “smoking gun”**  
   Archaeologists and engineers have proposed many workable methods (straight ramps, spiral ramps, zigzagging ramps, levering systems, water lubrication, internal tunnels, etc.). Each can explain some evidence, but each also has weaknesses when scaled up to the height and weight of the Great Pyramid’s 2.3 million blocks, some weighing over 15 tons. The challenge isn’t finding *a* way to move and lift stones; it’s proving which combination was actually used in which phase, on that specific monument.

3. **Lost organic materials**  
   Many construction aids would have been made of wood, rope, and mudbrick—materials that rot or erode over millennia. Wooden sledges, rollers, and scaffolding have long since disappeared, leaving only indirect evidence like wear patterns on stone surfaces or the remains of ramp foundations. We can infer, but we can’t fully reconstruct.

4. **The builders adapted as they went**  
   The Great Pyramid wasn’t built with a single master plan and one method from start to finish. Evidence suggests the design evolved (the internal chambers changed), and the construction team likely improvised different lifting strategies at different heights. The lower courses might have been dragged up straight earthen ramps, while the upper courses—where ramp volume would become impossible—may have used a compact spiral ramp or even lever systems on the stepped inner core. Untangling that layered history is extremely difficult.

5. **The scale and precision are still astonishing**  
   Even if we know the physics is possible, the sheer logistics—quarrying, transporting, and placing a block every few minutes for 20+ years with copper tools and human muscle—are mind-boggling. The precision of the casing stones (now mostly gone) and the alignment to true north within a fraction of a degree suggest a level of surveying and organizational skill that we can explain only in theory. The “how” isn’t just about brute force; it’s about the system, and the system’s details elude us in part because no modern society has ever tried to replicate the entire process at full scale with authentic ancient tools.

6. **New discoveries keep rewriting the story**  
   In recent decades, we’ve found the workers’ village, the papyrus diaries of a leader named Merer (who transported Tura limestone by boat), traces of a complex water-wet ramp system in an ancient quarry, and internal voids in the Great Pyramid detected by muon scanning. Each find reveals a new piece but often deepens the puzzle by suggesting methods we hadn’t considered or confirming that multiple techniques were in play simultaneously.

So it’s not that we’re clueless—we know the ancient Egyptians used copper chisels, dolerite hammers, ramps, sledges, boats, and enormous manpower. The enduring gap is the exact choreography of how those elements fitted together to produce such a precise mountain of stone. Until someone digs up a detailed architect’s report or we find a fully intact ramp system, some of the fine-grained details will remain a matter of informed debate.
