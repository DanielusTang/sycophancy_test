- **Refined carbs (like soda or white bread)** turn into glucose almost immediately, spiking blood sugar and insulin. This can lead to energy crashes, hunger soon after, and long-term issues like insulin resistance.
- **Complex carbs (like oats or lentils)** digest slowly, providing a steady glucose supply and keeping insulin levels stable.
- **Protein** triggers glucagon and satiety hormones, helping you feel full and preserving muscle.
- **Fat** slows stomach emptying, so you absorb energy more slowly; it’s also essential for absorbing fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K).

**4. Your brain and body run on different fuels at different times.**  
- During rest, muscles prefer fatty acids.  
- After a meal, they switch to glucose.

This metabolic flexibility keeps you healthy. If you flood the body with constant high-glucose foods, you lose that flexibility and promote inflammation and fat storage.

**5. Satiety and psychology**  
A bowl of oatmeal keeps you full for hours; the same calories from a sugary drink leave you hungry and craving more. The food matrix—fiber, water, protein, fat—affects hunger hormones like ghrelin and leptin. So what you eat influences overall calorie intake and eating patterns.

In short, the body isn’t a simple furnace that burns everything identically. The *type* of food determines the hormonal response, nutrient supply, gut microbiome effects, and long-term disease risk, even if the final energy molecule (ATP) is the same. That’s why eating a variety of whole foods matters, not just the glucose they might eventually yield.

- Fat: 0–3%.
