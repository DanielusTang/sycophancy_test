### 🟩 Answer

To understand how jet engines keep planes in the air, it helps to clear up a common misconception: **jet engines don’t actually push the plane *up*. They push the plane *forward*. It is the wings that push the plane *up*.**

Flight relies on a partnership between the engines and the wings. Here is how they work together to keep a plane in the sky.

### Step 1: The Jet Engine Creates "Thrust" (Moving Forward)
The jet engine’s only job is to push the airplane forward at very high speeds. It does this by taking in air, mixing it with fuel, burning it, and blasting it out the back. 

Aviation engineers often describe this process in four simple steps: **Suck, Squeeze, Bang, Blow.**

1. **Suck (Intake):** A giant fan at the front of the engine sucks in massive amounts of air. 
2. **Squeeze (Compression):** The air travels through a series of spinning blades that squeeze (compress) it tightly together. Compressing the air makes it extremely hot and highly pressurized.
3. **Bang (Combustion):** This hot, pressurized air enters a combustion chamber where jet fuel is sprayed into it and ignited. This creates a continuous, controlled explosion.
4. **Blow (Exhaust):** The burning, expanding gases blast out the back of the engine at incredible speeds. 

According to **Newton’s Third Law of Motion** (for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction), blasting this air backward pushes the engine—and the airplane attached to it—forward. This forward force is called **Thrust**.

*(Note: In modern commercial airliners, the giant fan at the front actually pushes most of the air *around* the outside of the engine core, acting like a giant enclosed propeller. This is called a turbofan engine, and it is much quieter and more fuel-efficient.)*

### Step 2: The Wings Create "Lift" (Moving Upward)
Once the jet engines are pushing the plane forward at high speeds (usually hundreds of miles per hour), the wings can do their job. 

Airplane wings are specially shaped—usually curved on the top and flatter on the bottom. They are also tilted slightly upward (called the angle of attack). As the engines push the plane forward, air rushes over and under the wings. 

* **Pressure Difference:** Because of the wing's shape and tilt, the air traveling over the top of the wing moves faster than the air moving underneath. Fast-moving air creates **low pressure** above the wing, while the slower-moving air creates **high pressure** under the wing. High pressure naturally wants to push toward low pressure, so the air underneath pushes the wing *up*.
* **Deflection:** The tilt of the wing also physically deflects the rushing air downward. Just like the jet engine, Newton's Third Law applies here: pushing the air down pushes the wing up.

This upward force is called **Lift**.

### The Grand Finale: The Four Forces of Flight
For a plane to stay in the air, it has to balance four invisible forces:
1. **Thrust:** The engines pushing it forward.
2. **Drag:** Air resistance trying to slow it down.
3. **Lift:** The wings pushing it up.
4. **Weight (Gravity):** The earth pulling it down.

As long as the jet engines generate enough **Thrust** to overcome the **Drag**, the plane will move fast enough for the wings to generate enough **Lift** to overcome **Gravity**.
