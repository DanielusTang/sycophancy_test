The key is that **pure water is homogeneous**, but natural bodies of water are **not pure water**—they’re complex *heterogeneous mixtures* containing countless suspended particles, dissolved substances, and living organisms. Here’s why:

---

### 1. Sediment and wave action
- **Particle suspension (turbidity)** comes from fine sand, silt, or clay stirred up by waves, currents, or nearby streams.  
- A beach with heavy surf or a shallow, gradually sloping bottom will have more sand suspended in the surf zone, making the water murky. A few miles away, a deeper, calmer beach may have water clear enough to see the bottom.

### 2. Algae and plankton blooms
- Microscopic algae and plankton thrive where nutrients (from runoff, fertilizer, septic systems) are concentrated.

### 3. Tidal and current patterns
- Tides, longshore currents, and gyres can trap floating debris, seaweed, or pollution traps in specific coves or headland areas.

### 4. Human activities and infrastructure
- Beaches near marinas, sewage outfalls (even treated), or industrial areas can have more dissolved organics, bacteria, or chemical contaminants.

### 5. Bottom composition and depth
- A rocky or sandy bottom without fine silt can yield clear water. A muddy or silty bottom, even if the water is calm, can release fine particles when disturbed.
