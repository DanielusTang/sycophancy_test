### 🟩 Answer

Because they don’t physically displace the air, they never generate the pressure pile‑up that leads to a shock wave.

The same is true for radio waves, X‑rays, and all other electromagnetic radiation.

So, when you flip a light switch, you don’t hear a sonic boom because **light isn’t an object trying to push through the air—it’s a different kind of phenomenon entirely**. The “speed limit” that creates sonic booms applies only to material things moving through a medium, not to light itself.
