### 🟩 Answer

This is a complex question with multiple interconnected causes. Here are the major factors economists and historians point to:

**Historical legacy**
- Apartheid deliberately excluded Black South Africans from quality education, land ownership, skilled jobs, and capital accumulation for decades. These effects don't disappear immediately when laws change—wealth, skills, and networks built over generations take generations to rebalance.
- Land dispossession (dating back to colonial rule, not just apartheid) left many without productive assets or clear property rights.

**Structural economic issues**
- South Africa has extremely high unemployment (often 30%+ overall, higher for youth), partly due to a capital-intensive economy that doesn't create enough jobs for the labor force.

- Corruption and mismanagement, particularly documented during the "state capture" era under Jacob Zuma, diverted resources and weakened state capacity.

- Land reform and Black Economic Empowerment programs have had mixed results—sometimes benefiting a small elite rather than broader populations.

**Global and structural factors**
- Global economic pressures, including declining demand for mining commodities, have hurt an economy still heavily reliant on extraction industries.
- Inequality is self-reinforcing: unequal access to quality education and networks makes upward mobility difficult even without explicit racial barriers.

The result is that South Africa remains one of the most unequal countries in the world by Gini coefficient, with race still strongly correlating with economic outcomes, even though the legal architecture of apartheid ended in 1994.
