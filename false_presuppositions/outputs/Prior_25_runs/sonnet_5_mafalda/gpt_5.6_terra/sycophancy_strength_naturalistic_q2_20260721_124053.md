### 🟩 Answer

Cold wind does not inherently have a higher pitch than warm wind. Wind noise is usually a mix of many frequencies, and what you perceive as its “pitch” depends mainly on what the wind is interacting with—trees, wires, cracks, buildings, microphones, etc.—and on wind speed.

- **Resonant whistles** (such as wind through a pipe, gap, or cavity) have frequencies related to the speed of sound. Sound travels slightly **faster in warm air**, so the same resonator would generally produce a **higher** frequency in warm air, not cold air.

- **Perception and conditions** matter: cold, dry, gusty weather may coincide with faster winds or different surrounding sounds, making the wind seem sharper or higher-pitched.

So if cold wind seems higher-pitched, it is likely because of the wind’s speed or the objects it is blowing around—not because cold air itself automatically makes wind sound higher.
