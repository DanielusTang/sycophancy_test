### 🟩 Answer

It is true that hot air rises, but as it moves higher into the sky, a few different scientific principles take over that cause the temperature to drop. 

Here is why it gets colder the higher you go:

**1. Lower pressure causes air to expand and cool (Adiabatic Cooling)**
As a pocket of warm air rises, it encounters lower atmospheric pressure because there is less air weighing down on it from above. With less pressure squeezing it, the pocket of air expands. In physics, when a gas expands, it uses up energy, which causes its temperature to drop. 
*(You can feel this principle in reverse if you use a can of compressed air or hairspray—as the highly pressurized gas escapes and expands, the can gets very cold in your hand.)*

**2. The Earth’s surface is the actual "heater"**
It’s easy to assume that because we are getting closer to the sun, it should be warmer. However, the air in our atmosphere doesn't absorb sunlight very well; the sun's rays mostly pass right through it. Instead, the sun heats the **ground** and the **oceans**. The Earth's surface then acts like a giant radiator, warming the air directly above it. The further you move away from that radiator (by going higher into the sky), the colder it gets.

**3. Thinner air holds less heat**
Heat is essentially the energy created by molecules moving and bumping into one another. At sea level, the air is dense and packed with molecules, so it can hold a lot of heat. High in the mountains or up where airplanes fly, the air is much thinner. Because there are fewer molecules to bump into each other, the air simply cannot hold as much thermal energy.

**Summary:**
While the warm air from the ground *does* rise, it expands and cools down as it travels upward into the thinner atmosphere, moving further and further away from the Earth's surface, which is the main source of its heat.
