### 🟩 Answer

Music in movies can be changed for several reasons, even when rights were initially secured. The key point is that music rights are not always permanent or universal. Here are the most common reasons:

1. **Licensing Expiration** – Many music licenses are time-limited (e.g., 5, 10, or 20 years). When the rights expire for a specific format (DVD, streaming, TV broadcast), the studio may need to renegotiate. If the cost is too high or the rights holder refuses, they substitute the song.

2. **Territorial Rights** – A song might be licensed for North America but not for Europe or Asia. For a global release, the studio may have to swap out a track to avoid legal issues in other regions.

3. **Home Video/Digital Release** – Theatrical licenses often don’t cover home video, streaming, or TV broadcasts. A song used in the cinema might need a separate, more expensive license for DVD or Netflix. If the studio can’t afford it, they replace it.

4. **Director’s or Studio’s Choice** – Sometimes a director uses a placeholder song (temp track) during editing, then fails to secure the real song later. Or a composer’s original score replaces a licensed pop song for creative consistency.

5. **Re-Releases and Remasters** – When a movie is re-released in a new format (4K remaster, anniversary edition), older music licenses may not carry over. The studio might need to renegotiate or change the music.

6. **Cover Version vs. Original** – The rights to a recording (by a specific artist) are separate from the rights to the composition (song itself). If the master recording is unavailable or too expensive, a cover may be used instead, which can sound different to the audience.

7. **Syncing Issues** – Occasionally, a license is secured but a dispute arises over a different use (e.g., a trailer or commercial), prompting the rights holder to revoke the license for the film itself.

So even if rights were "secured" for the original release, they often are not secured for every future version, region, or medium. Changing the music is a pragmatic move to avoid lawsuits or excessive costs.

- Re-license the song to other movies, ads, or games in the future.

### 3. Budget versus risk calculation
