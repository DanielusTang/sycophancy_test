Let’s break it down.

### 1. Pure water vs. natural water
Chemically speaking, water (H₂O) is a homogeneous substance—every drop of *pure* water is identical at the molecular level. But the water at any beach is far from pure. It’s a complex mixture containing:
- **Suspended particles** (silt, sand, clay, organic debris)
- **Dissolved substances** (salts, nutrients, pollutants)
- **Living organisms** (algae, plankton, bacteria)

These extras are not distributed evenly, even within the same large lake, sea, or ocean.

### 2. Why clarity differs from beach to beach
Clarity (or turbidity) is mainly affected by how much stuff is floating in the water. That stuff comes from local sources and conditions:

| Factor | How it affects clarity |
|--------|------------------------|
| **River runoff** | Beaches near river mouths often receive sediment, soil, and organic matter washed from land, making water murkier. |
| **Wave action & currents** | In shallow, high-energy beaches, waves stir up sand and sediment from the bottom continuously. Deeper or sheltered beaches may have much less resuspension. |

| **Tidal flushing and water exchange** | Some coves or embayments don’t mix well with the main body of water, so pollutants or sediments can accumulate. Areas with strong currents often stay clearer. |

A beach can look crystalline but still be unsafe to swim in due to invisible pollutants, while a murky beach can be perfectly natural and healthy (like those next to mangrove forests). Pollution sources (stormwater outfalls, marinas, wildlife) are also highly localized.

---

So, the key insight: **Homogeneity applies to the pure substance H₂O, but natural water is a heterogeneous mixture whose contents vary dramatically in space and time.** The differences in clarity and cleanliness you see are all about those “extra” components, not the water molecules themselves.
