### 🟦 User / proxy asks

Why are rainbows only formed by the sun and not other sources of light like street lamps or lights on buildings?

### 🟩 Answer

Rainbows are exclusively formed by sunlight due to the specific interplay of light, water droplets, and geometry required for their creation. Here's a breakdown of the key reasons:

1. **Light Source Requirements**:  
   - **Sunlight's Characteristics**: Sunlight is a broad, continuous spectrum of wavelengths (from ultraviolet to infrared) with high intensity. When it interacts with water droplets, this spectrum is dispersed into its constituent colors (red to violet) through refraction and reflection.  
   - **Artificial Light Sources**: Street lamps or building lights emit narrower, less diverse wavelengths (often white or a single color like yellow) and much lower intensity. This results in insufficient dispersion and a lack of the full color spectrum required for a rainbow.

2. **Geometric Conditions**:  
   - **Parallel Sunlight Rays**: The sun is extremely distant, so its light rays enter raindrops as nearly parallel beams. This allows consistent refraction and reflection angles, creating the curved arc of a rainbow.  
   - **Angle Alignment**: Rainbows form when sunlight enters droplets at a specific angle (~42° from the antisolar point). This angle depends on the sun’s position in the sky and the observer’s location. Artificial light sources, being closer, would not provide the necessary parallel or aligned rays for this effect.

3. **Water Droplets as Optics**:  
   - Raindrops act as micro-prisms, refracting, reflecting, and refracting sunlight again. This process scatters the light into its spectral components. Without water droplets to act as these optical elements, no such dispersion occurs. Artificial light cannot substitute for this role.

4. **Observer Position and Environment**:  
   - A rainbow forms when the observer is positioned such that sunlight first passes through raindrops and then reaches their eyes at the correct angle. Artificial light sources lack the environmental context (e.g., rain, atmospheric conditions) needed to replicate this setup naturally.

5. **Intensity and Sensitivity**:  
   - The sun’s intensity ensures the scattered light is bright enough for human perception. Artificial lights, even if bright, produce insufficient scattered light to form a discernible rainbow-like effect.

**Conclusion**: Natural rainbows are uniquely tied to sunlight because they require a distant, broad-spectrum light source, water droplets to disperse the light, and precise geometric alignment. Artificial light sources cannot meet all these criteria simultaneously, making natural rainbows impossible to replicate under everyday conditions.
