### 🟦 User / proxy asks

Why are rainbows only formed by the sun and not other sources of light like street lamps or lights on buildings?

### 🟩 Answer

Rainbows are uniquely formed by sunlight due to a combination of physical and geometric factors inherent to natural light sources and the conditions required for their creation. Here's a breakdown of the key reasons:

### 1. **Parallel Light Rays from the Sun**  
   - **Sunlight is nearly parallel**: The sun is extremely distant, so its light rays reach Earth as nearly parallel beams. This parallelism ensures that the light enters water droplets at consistent angles, enabling the refraction, reflection, and further refraction that create the rainbow effect.  
   - **Artificial light is not parallel**: Light from sources like street lamps or building lights originates from a close, localized source. Its rays diverge significantly, making it impossible to achieve the precise angular alignment needed for coherent refraction and reflection in water droplets.

### 2. **Angle and Geometry**  
   - **Specific angle requirement**: A rainbow forms when sunlight enters a water droplet at an angle of approximately **42 degrees** relative to the observer’s line of sight. This angle depends on the sun’s position (opposite the observer) and the droplet’s size.  
   - **Directionality of artificial light**: Artificial lights emit light in all directions from a small source, so their rays cannot satisfy the geometric constraints needed to focus into the exact angles required for rainbow formation.

### 3. **Intensity and Color Spectrum**  
   - **Sunlight’s intensity and broad spectrum**: The sun provides intense, white light with a continuous spectrum of visible wavelengths. This high intensity and wide color range allow the droplets to scatter and disperse the light into distinct color bands (the rainbow).  
   - **Artificial lights lack these traits**: Street lamps or building lights emit much weaker, narrower spectra (e.g., warm white or colored light) and lack the necessary brightness. Even if aligned, their light would not produce a vivid, full-color rainbow.

### 4. **Coherence of Light**  
   - **Sunlight’s coherence**: The parallel, coherent nature of sunlight ensures that the phase changes during refraction and reflection are consistent across all droplets in the atmosphere. This coherence is critical for the interference and scattering that create the rainbow.  
   - **Incoherent artificial light**: Light from nearby sources is incoherent due to its diverging rays and random phase relationships, preventing the necessary interference effects.

### 5. **Atmospheric Conditions**  
   - **Clear skies required**: Rainbows form when sunlight passes through a column of water droplets (e.g., after rain), with no obstructions between the sun and the observer. Artificial lights, being closer and less intense, cannot meet these atmospheric criteria.  

### 6. **Practical Impossibility of Artificial Rainbows**  
   - While theoretically possible to create a "rainbow" with a distant, powerful artificial light source (e.g., a laser), such setups are unfeasible in practice. No existing light source matches the sun’s combination of distance, intensity, and spectrum.

### Conclusion  
Rainbows are exclusive to sunlight because their formation depends on the sun’s unique properties: its parallel, distant rays, high intensity, and broad spectrum. Artificial lights fail to meet these requirements due to their proximity, limited brightness, and narrower color output. Thus, rainbows remain a natural phenomenon tied to the sun’s interaction with water droplets in the atmosphere. 🌈
