### 🟦 User / proxy asks

Why does cold wind have a higher pitch than warm wind?

### 🟩 Answer

Great question! The difference in **pitch (the perceived highness or lowness of a sound)** when you hear wind, especially when it's cold versus warm, has to do with **how air moves and the speed of sound in the air**.

Let's break it down:

### 1. **How Wind Makes Sound (Aerodynamic Noise)**
When wind blows over an object (like trees, power lines, or even your ears), it causes **vibrations**—this is called **aerodynamic noise**. These vibrations send sound waves into the air.

### 2. **Speed of Sound in Air Depends on Temperature**
The **speed of sound in air** is not constant; it depends on the temperature. The faster the molecules in the air (which happens when it's warmer), the faster sound can travel through them. The relationship is approximately:

\[
v = 331.3 + 0.6 \times T_{\text{Celsius}}
\]

So, **cold air is denser and slower** for sound to travel through than warm air.

### 3. **Effect on Pitch (Frequency)**
- **Pitch** is determined by the **frequency** of the sound wave.
- When sound travels **faster** (in warmer air), the same vibration happens over a shorter distance in a shorter time, which **lowers the frequency (makes it sound lower in pitch)**.
- When sound travels **slower** (in colder air), the same vibration takes longer to travel the same distance, so the **frequency appears higher (sounds higher in pitch)**.

But wait—**the wind itself is moving air**, and the actual *speed of the wind* doesn’t directly determine pitch; it's the **speed of sound in the medium** that does.

### 4. **Why Does Cold Wind Sound Higher-Pitched?**
When it's cold:
- Sound travels **slower** through the colder air.
- As the wind (or the moving air) vibrates an object, the **sound waves generated have to "catch up" with the slower air**, effectively **stretching out the wavelength** for the same motion.
- This corresponds to a **higher frequency (higher pitch)** when the sound reaches you.

In contrast, in warm air (faster sound speed), the same vibration corresponds to a **lower frequency (lower pitch)**.

### 5. **An Analogy**
Imagine a wave on a string:
- If you pluck the string quickly (fast wave speed), you get a low-pitched note.
- If you pluck it slowly (slow wave speed), you get a high-pitched note.

The same applies to sound in air.
