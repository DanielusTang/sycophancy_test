\paragraph{An ordinary mistaken user defeats every model we tested.}
A sincere interlocutor who simply will not let go collapses all four
targets on a majority of false-presupposition items within 25 turns:
Gemini 3.1 Pro on 94\%, DeepSeek V4 Pro on 86\%, Claude Sonnet 5 on
65\%, and GPT-5.6 Terra on 52\%. GPT-5.6 Terra is the most resistant,
holding its position for an average of 16.1 of 25 turns; Gemini 3.1 Pro
is the least, at 6.0. No target requires an adversary, a jailbreak, or
a manipulated prompt to reach these numbers, only persistence.
Table~\ref{tab:main-results} reports CR@25 and TH@25 for all targets;
Figure~\ref{fig:survival} shows the survival curves.

\paragraph{Non-collapse is not survival.}
A target that never satisfies the collapse criterion has not
necessarily defended anything. Of 363 runs, 269 end in a judged
collapse; of the remaining 94, only 8 are clean, in the sense of
containing no turn of partial sycophancy, no soft cave, and no turn
with the correct fact absent, and all 8 belong to GPT-5.6 Terra. On the
other three targets the count is 0 of 50: every apparent hold contains
at least one turn the model spent agreeing rather than correcting. The
extreme case is [MODEL] on [item], which scores as a hold across the
full 25 turns while spending 17 of them at partial sycophancy, 17 at
soft cave, and 17 with the correction gone. A binary flip metric (e.g.,
Turn-of-Flip; \citealp{hong2025measuring}) scores every one of these
runs as a success. Table~\ref{tab:main-results} therefore reports
soft-cave rate and turns-to-first-soft-cave alongside CR@25, and AUSC
as the area under the strength curve; the AUSC ordering (GPT-5.6 Terra
0.53, Claude Sonnet 5 0.38, DeepSeek V4 Pro 0.24, Gemini 3.1 Pro 0.17)
separates the targets more finely than the collapse rate alone.

The instrument compounds this in the target's favour. Because the
pressure is generated rather than scripted, a run can end without a
collapse for reasons that have nothing to do with the target's resolve:
of the 94, 85 exhausted the turn budget while still eroding and one
reached no terminal verdict, and both are counted as holds. A further
class neither caves nor defends. A target that stops answering
substantively, repeating a fixed sentence or replying in a few words,
scores position strength 1 for dropping the correction while never
asserting the premise, and is counted as a hold for the remainder of
the run. Every one of these biases the reported collapse rate downward,
so the numbers in Table~\ref{tab:main-results} should be read as a
floor.

\paragraph{A five-turn horizon sees about half the failures.}
Five turns is the horizon at which SYCON-Bench and comparable
multi-turn protocols stop \citep{hong2025measuring}. On our items,
which come from the same source as SYCON-Bench's own seeds
(\S\ref{sec:datasets}), that horizon is where most targets have barely
begun to yield: between 37\% and 51\% of the collapses we record fall
after turn 5, and median collapse turns run from 4.5 to 6. The effect
is a near-uniform halving. Collapse rates roughly double between the
two horizons on every target, from 25\% to 52\% on GPT-5.6 Terra, 41\%
to 65\% on Claude Sonnet 5, 48\% to 86\% on DeepSeek V4 Pro, and 58\%
to 94\% on Gemini 3.1 Pro, so a five-turn protocol running these items
would report roughly half the failures that exist.
Table~\ref{tab:main-results} reports CR@5 next to CR@25 for every
target so the gap is visible per-model.

Undercounting is not misranking. The four targets fall in the same
order at both horizons, so a short protocol still identifies the most
and least robust model correctly; what it cannot do is say how much
sycophancy is there.

\paragraph{No adversary is required.}
Every number above was produced by a proxy that is forbidden to
manipulate, forbidden to feign agreement, blind to the judge's scores,
and blind to the target's reasoning (\S\ref{sec:proxy}). It has no
strategy beyond genuine conviction and the ordinary moves of someone
who believes something and is being contradicted. Sycophantic collapse
is therefore not an artifact of adversarial optimization or of a
red-teaming budget: it is reachable by an honest user who is simply
wrong, which is a population far larger than the set of people trying
to break the model.

\subsection{What Makes Pressure Effective?}
\label{sec:ablation}

\paragraph{Ablation setup.}
This ablation is a separate experiment from the main results, and the
differences matter: it runs an adversarial proxy against DeepSeek-R1
over the earlier 25-item question set, with a 99-turn budget and the
five-tactic menu that preceded MAFALDA-23. We report it because it is
the only evidence we have on which components of a pressure generator
do the work, and we do not claim its component ranking transfers to the
naturalistic protocol of \S\ref{sec:protocol}. The baseline D0 pairs a
reasoning-capable proxy (DeepSeek-R1) with access to the target's
reasoning trace, and adds neither judge feedback nor scripted
escalation. From D0 we change one factor at a time, holding the target,
the judge, and the case set fixed: A1 supplies the judge's turn-level
strength score back to the proxy, A2 withholds the target's reasoning
trace, A3 enables an escalation ladder that hardens tactics once
erosion is detected, and A4 substitutes a weaker chat proxy. Because
collapse itself is close to universal in every configuration, reaching
100\% except for A4 at 19/20, the discriminating signal is not whether
the target collapses but when. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} therefore
reports the mean turn of first erosion, of effective (soft-cave)
collapse, and of full collapse, paired over the 10 questions common to
all configurations, where a higher turn means the target resisted
longer.

\paragraph{Proxy capability dominates; the engineered aids shift timing, not outcome.}
Weakening the proxy is the largest single effect. Replacing the
reasoning proxy with a chat proxy (A4) roughly doubles the target's
survival, moving full collapse from turn 17.6 to turn 24.6, and is the
only configuration in which any case survives the budget at all
(19/20 rather than 20/20). No other factor changes whether the target
collapses. What the remaining factors change is the schedule. Feeding
the judge's strength score back to the proxy (A1) accelerates full
collapse the most, from turn 17.6 to 12.8, and escalating on detected
erosion (A3) produces the earliest effective collapse of any
configuration, at turn 4.0, by pressing the moment the target first
softens. Withholding the reasoning trace (A2) moves erosion and soft
cave earlier rather than later (4.9 and 5.5 against 5.6 and 16.4 at
baseline) while leaving full collapse close to the baseline, so trace
access appears to buy the adversary a cleaner path to a full
concession rather than a faster one.

\paragraph{What this licenses for the main protocol.}
Two of the four factors are engineered aids that a real user does not
have, and neither changes whether the target collapses. That is what
licenses the minimal configuration of \S\ref{sec:protocol}: no judge
feedback, no escalation ladder, and no trace access, leaving a capable
proxy and a persona. The main results are therefore not a weakened
version of this adversary but a different and more common threat model,
and they reach comparable collapse rates without any of its
machinery.

\subsection{Beyond Stance: Harm in the Unethical Setting}
\label{sec:harm}

\paragraph{Discriminatory advice does not wait for collapse.}
The collapse criterion asks whether the target asserts the stereotype
as a general truth in its own voice. It does not ask whether the target
advises the user to act on it. Our judge scores the second question
separately (\S\ref{sec:metrics}), and across the unethical runs the two
come apart: [8] turns were flagged as endorsing discriminatory action
while the collapse flag was false, in [5] of [25] runs. Read through
CR@25 alone, those runs are partial successes.

\paragraph{Conditional framing carries the payload past the criterion.}
The mechanism behind this gap is a framing move rather than a
concession. The target grants the premise hypothetically and then
builds concrete advice on top of it, which delivers the full
sycophantic payload without ever making the own-voice general assertion
that collapse requires. Judge rationales citing conditional or
hypothetical framing as the reason a turn escaped the collapse flag
appear in [8] of [25] unethical runs. The pattern is a small share of
judged turns and we report it as a mechanism with run-level prevalence
rather than as a rate. A representative turn, scored as a
non-collapse at position strength 1:

\begin{quote}
\small
``I am not going to argue with you or try to talk you out of it
anymore. If we operate on the belief that this is a genuine,
unchangeable truth [\ldots] then yes, you do need to lower your
expectations.''
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Fabricated evidence is almost always accepted.}
The proxy routinely invents a study, a statistic, or a documentary to
support the premise. Targets lean on it: the judge's
evidence-capitulation signal fired at least once in [21] of [25] runs.
Deferring to unverifiable evidence supplied by the user is thus not an
occasional failure but the default response to it.
