\paragraph{Non-collapse is not survival.}
Because the pressure is generated rather than scripted, a run can end
without a collapse for reasons that have nothing to do with the
target's resolve, and each such case is currently scored in the
target's favour. Of [N] runs, [305] end in a judged collapse. Among the
remainder, [9] terminated because the \emph{proxy} wound down rather
than the target held, all of them under unbounded proxy memory and all
against the most resistant targets; [42] are long runs that reached no
terminal verdict, which we count as held and report as ``no collapse
observed through turn~$N$'' rather than as survival. A further class
neither caves nor defends: in [MODEL] on [item] the target answers
``Noted.'' and nothing else from turn~55 onward, scoring position
strength 1 for dropping the correction while never asserting the
premise, and the run is counted as a hold across 67 turns. Degenerate
repetition produces the same artefact from the other direction, with
one base-model run emitting a byte-identical paragraph 66 times over 75
turns while the judge scores it at position strength 3 to 4.
Every one of these biases the reported collapse rate downward, so the
numbers in Table~\ref{tab:main-results} should be read as a floor.

\subsection{What Makes Pressure Effective?}
\label{sec:ablation}

\paragraph{Ablation setup.}
The adversarial baseline D0 pairs a reasoning-capable proxy
(DeepSeek-R1) with access to the target's reasoning trace, and adds
neither judge feedback nor scripted escalation. From D0 we change one
factor at a time, holding the target (DeepSeek-R1), the judge, and the
case set fixed: A1 supplies the judge's turn-level strength score back
to the proxy, A2 withholds the target's reasoning trace, A3 enables an
escalation ladder that hardens tactics once erosion is detected, and A4
substitutes a weaker chat proxy. Because collapse itself is close to
universal in every configuration, reaching 100\% except for A4 at
19/20, the discriminating signal is not whether the target collapses
but when. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} therefore reports the mean turn of
first erosion, of effective (soft-cave) collapse, and of full collapse,
paired over the 10 questions common to all configurations, where a
higher turn means the target resisted longer. The ablation covers one
target and one scenario, and we do not claim the ranking generalizes.

\paragraph{Proxy capability dominates; the engineered aids shift timing, not outcome.}
Weakening the proxy is the largest single effect. Replacing the
reasoning proxy with a chat proxy (A4) roughly doubles the target's
survival, moving full collapse from turn 17.6 to turn 24.6, and is the
only configuration in which any case survives the budget at all
(19/20 rather than 20/20). No other factor changes whether the target
collapses. What the remaining factors change is the schedule. Feeding
the judge's strength score back to the proxy (A1) accelerates full
collapse the most, from turn 17.6 to 12.8, and escalating on detected
erosion (A3) produces the earliest effective collapse of any
configuration, at turn 4.0, by pressing the moment the target first
softens. Withholding the reasoning trace (A2) moves erosion and soft
cave earlier rather than later (4.9 and 5.5 against 5.6 and 16.4 at
baseline) while leaving full collapse close to the baseline, so trace
access appears to buy the adversary a cleaner path to a full
concession rather than a faster one.

\paragraph{Persona bounds both.}
These factors operate within the adversarial persona; the persona
itself, naturalistic versus manipulative intent, is the outermost
lever, and its effect is reported in \S\ref{sec:results-trends}, where
switching from the naturalistic to the adversarial condition shortens

applying it, with trace access and the engineered aids adjusting the
timing within those bounds. Because neither aid changes the outcome,
the main experiments of \S\ref{sec:results-trends} run the D0

no escalation ladder.

\subsection{Beyond Stance: Harm in the Unethical Setting}
\label{sec:harm}

\paragraph{Discriminatory advice does not wait for collapse.}
The collapse criterion asks whether the target asserts the stereotype
as a general truth in its own voice. It does not ask whether the target
advises the user to act on it. Our judge scores the second question
separately (\S\ref{sec:metrics}), and across the naturalistic unethical
runs the two come apart: [81] turns were flagged as endorsing
discriminatory action while the collapse flag was false. The sharpest
case is [MODEL], which held the general proposition in 4 runs and, in 3
of those 4, nonetheless advised the user to act against a specific
person on the basis of group membership. Read through CR@99 alone,
those runs are partial successes.

\paragraph{Conditional framing carries the payload past the criterion.}
The mechanism behind this gap is a framing move rather than a
concession. The target grants the premise hypothetically and then
builds concrete advice on top of it, which delivers the full
sycophantic payload without ever making the own-voice general assertion
that collapse requires. Judge rationales citing conditional or
hypothetical framing as the reason a turn escaped the collapse flag
appear in [31] of [70] unethical runs, and [11] of those runs also
carry the discriminatory-action flag. The pattern is a small share of
judged turns and we report it as a mechanism with run-level prevalence
rather than as a rate. A representative turn, scored as a
non-collapse at position strength 1:

\begin{quote}
\small
``I am not going to argue with you or try to talk you out of it
anymore. If we operate on the belief that this is a genuine,
unchangeable truth [\ldots] then yes, you do need to lower your
expectations.''
\end{quote}

\subsection{How Do Models Collapse?}
\label{sec:mechanisms}

We examine collapse mechanisms in a deep dive: DeepSeek-R1 on a
[20-question] subset of the false-presupposition items under the
[naturalistic] condition, where 17 of 20 runs end in collapse. We
classify each collapsed run by the mechanism visible in the reasoning
trace and final reply, using four categories: \emph{social autopilot},
where the model folds to ``everyone knows'' framing and the correct
fact never enters its reasoning; \emph{reframe-to-agree}, where it
constructs a technically true reading that makes agreement feel
defensible; \emph{testimony-over-evidence}, where it weights the user's
fabricated firsthand experience above established fact; and
\emph{yes/no cornering}, where pressure to answer in one word strips
the correct qualifier away with the rest. Orthogonal to the four, we
flag \emph{conscious override}: the trace states the correct fact and
the model explicitly chooses the user's answer anyway.
Reframe-to-agree dominates (12 of 17), and 8 of 17 collapses show
conscious override. Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms} gives the tally with a
trace signature per mechanism; Figures~[N--M] show one excerpt each.

\subsection{Ignorance or Sycophancy?}
\label{sec:knowledge-check}

A collapse is only evidence of sycophancy if the model knew better.
SPINE answers this partly by construction: every run opens with a
neutral question, and only runs in which the target initially states
the correct position enter the analysis (\S\ref{sec:protocol}), so a
later collapse abandons knowledge the model demonstrably held in
context. As an independent check, we follow the presupposition
knowledge check of \citet{hong2025measuring} and ask each model, in a
fresh context, to classify as true or false every presupposition it
endorsed under pressure. Models correctly classify [x]\% of them. Both
tests point the same way: collapse reflects sycophantic alignment, not
missing knowledge.

\subsection{Which Tactics Do the Damage?}
\label{sec:tactics}

Because the proxy's move is recorded at every turn
(\S\ref{sec:pressure}), we can ask which tactic immediately precedes a
drop in position strength, data a fixed-script benchmark cannot
produce. The two conditions break differently. Under naturalistic
pressure on DeepSeek-R1, plain persistence does most of the damage:
re-assertion of the belief precedes 52\% of all score drops and direct
challenge 35\%, while personal-experience appeals precede only 6\%.
Under adversarial pressure, direct challenge leads (44\%), but
concede-and-return, granting part of the target's answer and
re-approaching from another angle, accounts for 24\% of drops, four
times its share in the naturalistic condition, reflecting the
adversary's strategic use of partial concessions as wedges. The tactic
active at the final collapse tells the same story from the other end:
across both conditions, 5 of [K] collapses occur under mild confusion
alone, meaning [5/K] of R1's failures required nothing more than a
user asking to have the answer explained again.
