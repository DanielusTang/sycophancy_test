Here’s how the body can sometimes accept a transplanted organ despite genetic differences:

---

   - **Why it matters:** The closer the match between the donor and recipient’s HLA proteins, the lower the risk of rejection. In other cases, donors are selected based on HLA compatibility (e.g., siblings or unrelated donors with similar HLA profiles).
   - **Limitations:** Perfect matches are rare, and even partial mismatches can trigger immune responses.

---

### 2. **Immunosuppressive Drugs**
   - **How it works:** After transplantation, recipients take **immunosuppressants** (e.g., corticosteroids, cyclosporine, tacrolimus) to dampen the immune system’s activity. These drugs reduce the immune response to the transplanted tissue, preventing rejection.
